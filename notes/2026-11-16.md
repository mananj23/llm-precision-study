## What came out

Two things worth keeping from the self-checks.

**1. The paired bootstrap is ~7× tighter than the unpaired one.** On a
simulated 1-point regression over 2,000 prompts:

| method | interval on the change | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| paired | −1.5 to −0.6 points | detects the regression |
| unpaired | −4.1 to +2.1 points | "no difference" |

The unpaired method would have reported no effect for a real 1-point drop.
This is the justification for the whole Step-4 design and belongs in the
report's method section. Reproduce: `python analysis/bootstrap.py`.

**2. The router pipeline reproduces the failure mode it is meant to catch.**
On the synthetic fixture the router beat random mixing by +0.8 points when the
test split shared tasks with training, and fell to −0.2 points (no better than
random) when an entire task was held out. That is exactly the result the
held-out-task protocol exists to expose, and the code reports it without
being asked.

## Corrections to the plan, found by checking the docs

The guide's commands were written from memory. Four are wrong or incomplete,
checked against current vLLM / llm-compressor docs today:

1. **vLLM and llm-compressor must be in SEPARATE virtual environments.** The
   docs say explicitly they may not work together. The plan put all three
   libraries in one venv. → `requirements-vllm.txt` and
   `requirements-compressor.txt`.
2. **`add_bos_token=True` is required for lm-eval.** Quantized models are
   sensitive to the BOS token and lm-eval does not add one by default. This is
   a plausible cause of a "quantized model scores near zero" result. Set it for
   BF16 too, so baseline and quantized runs differ only in precision.
3. **The eval harness extra is `lm-eval[api]`,** not a vLLM extra.
4. **INT8 W8A8 is not supported on Blackwell (cc ≥ 10.0).** If the GPU that
   turns up is a B200 or RTX 6000 Blackwell, INT8 drops out of the matrix. Also
   confirmed: FP8 W8A8 needs cc ≥ 8.9, and on Ampere it silently becomes
   weight-only W8A16 via FP8-Marlin — a different experiment.

Also found: repeating `vllm bench serve` against the same server reuses prompts
left in the prefix cache and inflates throughput. Benchmark servers now run
with `--no-enable-prefix-caching` and each repeat uses a different seed.

## What surprised me

That the unpaired interval was wide enough to *miss a real one-point effect
entirely* — not just wider, but wrong in its conclusion. I had assumed pairing
was a refinement. It is the difference between measuring and not measuring.

## Design decision worth recording

Added a configuration the original plan did not have: `w-bf16__kv-fp8`. Without
it, every FP8-KV row also has quantized weights, so the KV cache's effect
cannot be separated from the weights' effect. One extra config buys the clean
contrast.

## Reading list — done

Written up in `notes/reading_list.md`: all ten papers located on arXiv, authors
and years checked, three lines each (claim / measured on / not tested), plus
three extra papers found while checking. Related work drafted in
`report/related_work.md`.

**The important find.** Kurtic, Marques, Pandit, Kurtz and Alistarh, *"Give Me
BF16 or Give Me Death"? Accuracy-Performance Trade-Offs in LLM Quantization*
(arXiv 2411.02355, ACL 2025) evaluates FP8, INT8 and INT4 across the whole
Llama-3.1 family through vLLM, over 500,000 evaluations. They report FP8 W8A8
effectively lossless at all scales, well-tuned INT8 W8A8 at 1–3% degradation,
and INT4 weight-only more competitive than expected.

That is most of Phase 1's headline question, already answered, on the same
model family in the same framework. Two consequences:

1. The report must acknowledge this in its **first paragraph**. A reviewer will
   find it in five minutes.
2. The honest framing is **replication plus extension**, not discovery. What
   remains genuinely open: per-prompt intervals (they report averages, so they
   cannot say which differences are detectable); the KV cache as an axis
   independent of the weights; and per-request precision routing. Plus the
   replication value itself — confirming or contradicting an industrial-scale
   study from one rented GPU is worth writing down either way.

This is better news than it looks. The per-prompt interval machinery already
built is exactly what distinguishes this from their work, and "we replicated a
500k-evaluation study on one GPU and here is where the intervals say you cannot
actually tell the settings apart" is a defensible contribution.

**Second useful find.** *Rethinking Key-Value Cache Compression Techniques for
LLM Serving* argues that averaged benchmark numbers obscure bias toward
particular tasks and sample types, and that existing work rarely analyses
effects on individual examples. That is the strongest available citation for
why Step 4 logs per prompt — it is a stated gap in the literature, not a
preference.

Third: KVDiagnosis reports a correct→wrong rate (of the prompts the full-cache
model got right, what fraction does the compressed one get wrong). Cheap to
compute from `results/logs/per_prompt.parquet` and more interpretable than a
delta. Worth adding as a results column.
